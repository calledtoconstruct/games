# Age and content lock

Date: 2026-10-18. This is the content lock for five browser games: Sudoku, Math, Word search, Flash cards, and Maze. It is not code. Bands are about 2–3, 4–5, 6–8, and 9+. Ids stay `tiny`, `little`, `shop`, and `rush`. Do not drop Rush. Do not add a fifth band. Visible menu names were customized per game on 2026-10-19. The age ranges in this note still use the old shared names as band labels. Do not put those shared names back on a menu.

Every picture is an original canvas drawing: one object, flat fills, high contrast, no letters, no logo, no downloaded file, no trademark, no character. A color chip is a flat disk, which is the one allowed non-object shape. Speech, where a band uses it, is on-device only. Nothing is recorded. Nothing is fetched.

Shared rules:

- A miss stays on the same challenge, or, in a maze, walks back out of a dead end. It does not buzz, subtract a life, stamp a word, or advance the sitting.
- No score, streak, rank, mistake total, or star rating on any band.
- No clock ends a round. Minute figures in the research are not cutoffs. Done ends a sitting early. Caps below are ceilings, not quotas.
- Menu controls (Done, Back, Another, Harder, Again, Backward) dwell longer than play targets. Play targets are the cells, cards, stones, and symbols.

## Sudoku

The game is Sudoku on every band: each symbol once in its row, once in its column, and once in its box. One solution. A singles-only solver must finish the board. Empties are spread across the boxes. Stop deleting clues at the given floor. Do not aim for a minimal puzzle.

On screen for every band: the grid, a tray of large symbols, Undo, and Hint. Hint lights one cell that is a single right now and pulses its tray symbol. It does not write the symbol. A given cell does not latch. A wrong look does nothing. After the board matches, Another appears. The banner is not a number, a time, or a rank.

Tiny Tots extra check: each of the four empty cells is a naked single on the opening board, before any fill. The child dwells an empty cell, then the one picture in the tray. That is the whole move.

Givens must include at least 3 of the 4 pictures on a 4×4, at least 5 of the 6 digits on a 6×6, and at least 8 of the 9 digits on a 9×9.

## Math

The game is one missing value in a three-part quantity. Older bands see it as an equation. Tiny Tots see the same three parts drawn in apples, because a written plus sign is not a fair 2–3 task. No keypad on any band. No compare problems, no two-step problems, no parentheses, no remainder. Math does not speak.

A wrong card leaves the problem as it was. That card ignores the pointer until the look leaves. The sitting ends at the cap or at Done.

Card rule, whenever the band uses numerals:

- One card is the correct value.
- One card is one away from it, if that neighbor is inside the band's range and not already used.
- When the blank is a part or a factor, one card is the two visible numbers added together, if that sum is inside the range and not already used.
- Fill any card still empty with another value inside the range.
- Cards are distinct. No negatives. No number the band never uses.

### Tiny Tots

On screen: apples only. No digit, no `+`, no `−`, no `=`. The apple is a red circle, a short brown stem, and one green leaf, no face. Every apple is identical. The two cards fill the lower screen with a wide gap.

The sitting is these four challenges, in this order. Cap 4.

| # | What the player sees | Cards, left then right | Correct card |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | A tray holding 1 apple, then an empty tray the same size | 1 apple; 2 apples | 1 apple |
| 2 | A tray holding 2 apples, then an empty tray | 2 apples; 3 apples | 2 apples |
| 3 | One row: 1 apple, then 1 empty apple outline | 2 apples; 1 apple | 2 apples |
| 4 | One row: 2 apples, then 1 empty apple outline | 3 apples; 2 apples | 3 apples |

Challenges 1 and 2 are the matching set: the empty tray is the blank, and the correct card copies the filled tray. Challenges 3 and 4 are one more: the outline is the blank, and the correct card is the row with that place filled. A correct card is drawn into the blank and stays. Counts are only 1, 2, and 3.

## Word search

Twenty-six original groups. Each group has a home band and is offered only there. Picking a group places it again from a new seed. The bank order stays the order listed here. The grid changes. Each answer string appears at most once. A bank dwell speaks the word and does not mark it found. Tiny Tots and Little Pals also show that word's picture on the chip. Shopkeeper and Rush chips are the uppercase word only.

The grid is uppercase. Directions are the only ones that can be marked. A mark is the first cell and the last cell, either order, not a dragged stroke. A segment that is not an answer does not buzz; that cell becomes the anchor. The puzzle stays up.

Right-only groups must share at least one cell (one word's ending against the next word's start on the same row). Bands that may go down or on a diagonal prefer an overlap ratio of at least 1.15 and do not throw away a unique grid only for missing it. Do not chase a board that is 80 percent full before filler.

Stored fallbacks for the young bands are below. Older groups ship a fallback from the same checker before play. Play never shows a grid that failed the one-span scan.

## Flash cards

The game is one word matched to one picture. The user's picture-then-word loop is the Shopkeeper and Rush default. It is not the Tiny Tots trial: those players do not read, so the choice is two pictures and the word is spoken. That is the same match, in the early direction.

One subject per sitting, chosen from a large menu. Foils come from that subject's list. Card positions shuffle at the start of a trial and stay put if the trial is retried. A wrong card does not reveal the answer and does not advance. Picture cards never speak. A printed word speaks at the moment its dwell commits, and only that word. Menus do not speak.

Printed words are lowercase, large, and high contrast. Pictures have no letters. After a correct commit, show that picture with its lowercase word and move on. No score.

| Band | Choices | What the player sees | Deck used as targets | Trials that end the sitting |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Tiny Tots | 2 pictures | Spoken word, then a picture. No printed choice | 6-word subject. Cap uses 8 targets | 8 |
| Little Pals | 3 pictures, or 3 word cards on a print trial | Spoken word, then a picture. A few trials flip | 8 words, or 6 for colors | 12 |
| Shopkeeper | 4 | Picture, then a printed word. About 1 in 3 flips | 10 | 16 |
| Rush | 4 | Half the trials flip, at random | 12, or the short Time deck | 16, or 8 for Time |

Schedule: shuffle the subject, append a second shuffle, and skip a boundary that would repeat the same word. Take targets from the front of that queue until the cap. Each word is a target at most twice.

Tiny Tots, on screen: a prompt tile on top and two picture cards under it. The tile is a flat dark speaker horn, no letters. It speaks the target once when the trial appears, and again if it is dwelled. Dwelling it does not select. The pictures have no words on them. Forbidden foil pairs, because those two look too much alike: dog-cat, bird-duck, milk-water, eye-nose, eye-mouth, nose-mouth, hand-foot. Any other word in the subject may be the foil.

Little Pals print trials: at most 3 of the 12, and only trials 4, 8, and 12, and only if that target was already a spoken target earlier in the sitting. Otherwise the trial stays spoken. On a print trial the prompt is the picture and the three cards are lowercase words. If the first commit on a print trial is wrong, count one miss. After two such misses, the rest of the sitting is spoken only. Colors are never a print trial.

Shopkeeper: each trial flips to a printed word prompt and four pictures with chance 1 in 3. The printed prompt is silent until they dwell it, and that dwell only speaks. Rush flips with chance 1 in 2 and uses the same hint rule.

Drawings: low yellow sun on a light blue sky. High sun on a bright sky. Low orange sun on a purple sky. Crescent moon on a dark blue sky, two small stars in that same drawing.

## Maze

The maze is generated. It is not a drawn picture and not a traced corridor. Walls are thick and dark brown. The floor is wide and cream. The token is a blue disk. The goal is a flat green ring. Stones are gold disks, about half the corridor width, with no label. Only the next one, two, or three stones are lit. A look at a wall, at a stone that is not lit, or at the floor does nothing. Dwelling a lit stone walks the token along the open floor to that stone. There is no jump. A dead end is a legal move. The stone behind the token lights, and the child dwells it to walk back. The maze does not reset.

One solution. Orthogonal. No loops, no braid, no sidewinder, no binary tree. Reject a maze whose lit stones are closer than one stone-width of floor, or whose stones sit on the screen edge. Painted stone diameter is at least about 3° for every band, and toward 5–6° for Tiny Tots and Little Pals.

Tiny Tots generation: a simple path on the 3×3 that turns exactly twice, so the walk is three straight runs. Straight cells collapse. Each run ends at a corner or at the goal. Reject a straight line, because that would be one dwell. From the token, only the end of the current run is lit.

Little Pals generation: growing tree, newest cell only, on a 4×4, plus two one-cell spurs. Reject a longer spur. Solution length 5 dwells after straights collapse.

Shopkeeper generation: growing tree, 70 percent newest cell and 30 percent random.

Rush generation: growing tree, half newest cell and half random.

No personal-best pace. No timer digits. Another after a finished maze, inside the cap above.
